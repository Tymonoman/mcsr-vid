# Hook style guide — amended 6 Oct 2026

Built from a competitor title study (340 videos, MCSR Matches/Replays/Vault + ours) and a 3-judge
panel (click, accuracy, risk) that scored 26 real matches' `hooks.ts` output. 4 of 52 candidate
hooks survived all three judges (the click judge alone kept 9, accuracy 25, risk 33); the panel's notes drove three code fixes the same night (below).
Read `src/pipeline/hooks.ts` and `src/shorts/videoPick.ts` for the live rules this amends.

## What the study found

- Plain "A vs B" and a stake hook land at the same median (1.00x channel-typical) across three
  competitors, n=150 and n=32 — a hook does not reliably move views over a plain title once
  channel size is matched. Matches the operator's own 1 Oct 2026 CTR reading (2.77% vs 2.59%,
  "a wash") independently.
- Question (1.24x, n=9) and hype (1.32x, n=6) are the only registers with any lift at all —
  too small to commit to, worth an occasional slot, not a rewrite.
- A channel's biggest outliers are usually a confound (Vault's Ludwig/Grian crossovers), not a
  hook-style win. Never credit naming for what a crossover did.
- Write hooks for legibility and trust, not a CTR lever. No title register reliably beats a plain
  one; the existing rules (no spoilers, no identity labels, a specific checkable fact) already
  point the right way.

## What never appears, and one thing that does

No competitor title replaces a real name with a slur, ethnicity or nationality label across all
340 videos — rare, which is exactly why the standing rule (`HOOK_DENIED_LABELS`) matters rather
than a one-off fix. What does appear, repeatedly, on MCSR Vault: a recurring nickname/epithet for
one player kept alongside their real name ("Unc vs GOAT", and directly "Unc vs TAS | Feinberg vs
Doogile" — the exact string that got "YN vs TAS" called out on X and cost a runner, per CLAUDE.md).
These titles perform fine for Vault (1.4–4.9x) — which is the trap: a nickname hook *can* work is
exactly why `hookLabelProblem` has to be unconditional, not performance-gated. A gender/identity
label as the whole hook ("GIRL??", MCSR Matches) is the same shape at a smaller scale.

## The panel: what survived, what didn't, and why

4 survivors, all "Decided by N seconds" under 3s (2.8, 1.6, 1.3 and 0.6 s). Zero "N seconds apart", "The lead changed N
times", "A M:SS lead, gone/N seconds swung it", "Not a single death between them" or any
question-form located hook ("Who's ahead at X?", "Can a flawless race still be this close?")
survived even once across the sample. The per-match breakdown with every judge's reasoning is in
the night's run (not duplicated here; ask the operator or check the session record) — the
takeaways that changed code:

1. **Grammar bug**: `seconds()` stripped a trailing ".0" and left the plural word, so a 1.03s
   margin shipped as "Decided by 1 seconds" — the exact "low quality" look viewers reported.
   Fixed: `secondsPhrase()` says "1 second" (singular), exact milliseconds under 100ms ("58
   milliseconds", not a rounded, 1.7x-overstated "0.1 seconds" — the channel's own past title was
   exact), and keeps the same "Decided by" subject through 10s instead of a bare "N seconds
   apart" (every instance of that phrasing was dropped for not saying what the number measured).
2. **False swing claims**: "A M:SS lead, gone" / "N seconds swung it" read `maxSwingMs`, the raw
   step between two splits' *signed* gaps — which fires exactly as readily when a leader simply
   pulls further ahead as when a lead is actually lost. Verified false on 5 real matches where the
   leader led wire to wire and won (13301662, 13333220, 13766294, and two more misstated by a
   different amount). Fixed: reads `metrics.splits` for the biggest lead a player held at a split
   they did not go on to win from, and asks "Can a M:SS lead survive?" — a question, never a flat
   claim, because "gone"/"swung it" named the eventual loser by elimination even without a name.
   The fix's first draft read every split `matchScore.ts` tracks, including the two the overlay
   panel never shows ("Bastion loot", "Blaze rod") — live `getMatch` verification on match
   13705580 caught it quoting the off-panel gap (2:36) instead of the panel-visible one (1:36,
   Blind travel) before it shipped. `OFF_PANEL_SPLITS` (`hooks.ts`) excludes both now, the same
   fix the accuracy judge asked for on the lead-change count.
3. **Lead-change count as a spoiler**: "The lead changed N times" both overcounted (it summed
   every split `matchScore.ts` tracks, including bastion loot and the blaze rod — the overlay
   panel never shows either) and, even corrected, lets a viewer work out who leads at the end by
   parity once they've counted the flips on screen. Fixed: the numberless question "Does the lead
   ever settle?" — same weight tiers (3+ changes outranks exactly 2), no count stated, so neither
   problem applies.
4. **"Reached" vs "killed"**: the DNF fallback said "One of them never reached the dragon", but a
   null `finishMarginMs` only means neither side logged a dragon kill — the loser can have entered
   the End and died fighting it (verified false on two real matches read as "never even got
   there"). Fixed: "killed", which is true by the field's own definition regardless of whether the
   loser reached the End.
5. **`hookProblem` disagreed with `spoilsTheResult`**: `videoPick.ts`'s own spoiler check didn't
   strip the "to win" idiom before testing for the bare word "win", so it silently rejected
   `hooks.ts`'s own highest-weighted candidate under 8 minutes ("A sub-N to win it") everywhere it
   would otherwise have won — on the dashboard's chip list (`shortFlow.ts`) and the Short's own
   `chipHook`/`extrasOf`. This alone explains most of the night's "filler" hooks: once fixed, the
   pace hook correctly outranks "Not a single death between them" and "One of them never killed
   the dragon" on every match where it applies, which was most of them.

## What was studied and deliberately NOT shipped

The panel also scored a set of located, teaser-style templates ("A death at the bastion, still
close", "The lead flips on blind travel", "Just 0.7s at the Nether enter?") drafted as candidates
for `hooks.ts`. Every single instance was rejected — split-sheet jargon ("Nether enter", "blind
travel") a non-speedrunner doesn't know, a promise ("still close") the video doesn't pay off, or a
flat comeback/flip claim that is a spoiler at low lead-change counts. None of these reached
`hooks.ts` or `videoPick.ts`; the evidence argues against adding them as flat statements at all.
If a located-fact hook is ever wanted, it must (a) use only words any Minecraft player knows
(fortress, bastion, stronghold, the End, the dragon — never "enter"/"travel" jargon), (b) be a
question, never a statement, whenever it touches a lead, and (c) only fire alongside a comparable
finish or an actually-measured count, never asserted on its own.

## The banned list (unchanged, still live)

- A runner's nickname, label or epithet — `hookLabelProblem`, denylist plus the "<x> vs <y>"
  mismatch check. Never performance-gated, "vs" or not, even a competitor's own working one.
- An identity label as the whole hook (gender, age, appearance) — same rule, same shape.
- The winner or any result word — `spoilsTheResult`, including "swept"/"sweep". A question is
  exempt. "To win" is stripped before the test in both `hooks.ts` and (as of tonight) `videoPick.ts`.
- A bracket seed, ladder rank or elo number — `HOOK_SEED_RANK_ELO`. Seed *type* ("village seed")
  is fine.
- The upset hook is a question, never a statement.

## Building a hook from match facts (unchanged order)

Finish margin → forfeit → lead changes (now a numberless question) → a lost lead (now a
question, gated on an actual reversal) → death count → pace ("A sub-N to win it") → rivalry →
fallback. Keep building from a checkable fact in `MatchMetrics`/`metrics.splits` — never a
keyword list, never an invented superlative ("flawless", "no mistakes": deaths=0 is not the same
as flawless, and a hunger reset still puts a death screen on the video even though it isn't a
real death).
