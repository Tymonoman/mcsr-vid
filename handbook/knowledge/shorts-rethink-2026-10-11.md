## Who is swiping

A Minecraft Shorts viewer: mostly 13–24, on a phone, sound on, deciding in about a second. They know Minecraft
inside out (piglins, beds exploding, water-bucket saves) and nothing about speedrunning (no "bastion route", "blind",
"elo", "splits"). They stay for a situation they get at once, stakes they feel (dying, losing everything, one heart,
seconds apart), a person reacting, something they have never seen done, and a payoff fast. They swipe on two small
screens, overlays full of numbers, a slow first second, or a caption in jargon. Our Shorts today are exactly that:
two stacked POVs at half size, nameplates and a race clock, a speedrun-fact hook, no face.

## Ideas

1. One player, one story: face on top, his game below, his voice — the streamer-clip layout gaming Shorts use.
2. Pick moments by emotion, not race stats: deaths, one heart, rage, laughter, luck, a skill nobody has seen. The
   signals are the streamer's voice ("oh my god") and chat bursts ("omg", "noooo").
3. A plain-words hook in the first second, four words or fewer, about the person ("HE WAS WINNING...", "1 HEART
   LEFT", "HE HAD EVERYTHING"), never jargon.
4. A "how is that legal" series: speedrun tricks ordinary players have never seen (the dragon killed with beds in
   seconds, a nether portal from buckets, piglin trades for pearls). They can travel beyond MCSR fans, and they
   explain the game, which also helps the YPP review.
5. The streamer's words as big captions.
6. 15–25 s, the turn before 10 s, end on a question, never the result.
7. A recurring title shape the feed can learn ("speedrunner vs piglins", "1 heart left").
8. Titles: the feeling first, then the names and #minecraft.
9. Two Shorts from a strong match once the format proves itself.
10. Judge a Short by Studio's "viewed vs swiped away" and engaged views; one that holds most viewers past the first
    second should escape the ~1.2K test batch.

## Small steps
