## How much are the competitors (MCSR Matches, MCSR Vault, MCSR Replays) earning per month?

Asked 29 Sept 2026 (07:17 UTC), answered 29 Sept 2026 (07:20 UTC). These are estimates from public data, not their real income.

**Are they monetised?** MCSR Matches has a membership "Join" button, which only YPP channels get, so it is in the Partner Program. Vault's and Replays' videos carry the same pre-roll ad slot that our own (non-YPP) video lacks, so they are very likely monetised too. That slot could also come from a copyright claim, so it is not proof.

| Channel | Views/month | Ad revenue/month | Monetised? |
|---|---|---|---|
| MCSR Matches | ~150–300K | ~$150–1,000, probably around $400 | Yes (Join button) |
| MCSR Vault | ~90–330K | ~$90–1,100, probably around $300 | Very likely |
| MCSR Replays | ~4–11K | ~$5–40 | Very likely |

**How the numbers were made**
- Views: every one of their videos' view counts was re-read on the morning of 29 Sept and compared with the counts saved the day before during the competitor research, over about 12.6 hours. Matches' catalogue gained about 3,150 views a day, plus 2,206 on the two videos posted since. Vault gained about 2,950 a day. Replays gained about 140 a day, though it has not posted since April. Each channel's total view count rose faster than that; YouTube updates totals in batches, so the totals were used as the top of each range.
- Revenue per 1,000 views: the YouTube skill's benchmark for gaming is $2–5, for a US audience. MCSR viewers are younger and spread across many countries, and most views are muted autoplay previews that earn little, so $1–3.50 was used.
- Shorts add essentially nothing: Vault gets about 27 Shorts views a day.

**Not counted:** Matches' memberships (the member count is not public), sponsors, and Super Thanks (none of the videos checked had the Thanks button).

**Peaks:** during Ludwig's run last winter Vault earned far more. In that January–February stretch its new videos alone drew about 850K views, likely $1,000–3,000 a month. Lifetime, if it has been monetised throughout, Vault's 2.97M views come to roughly $3,000–10,000.

**What it means for us:** Matches posts plain two-POV replays, much like ours, and YouTube still accepted it into YPP. That is a good sign for our own review, where reused content was the worry. At our then ~55K views a month we would earn roughly $55–190 a month once monetised, and we were about 102 days from the first tier. **Two facts that shape the answer**

| When (at the pace of 29 Sept) | What unlocks | Estimated money |

| ~9 Jan 2027, expanded tier at 500 subs | Memberships, Super Thanks, Super Chat. No ads. | Roughly $0–30 a month at our size. The Ko-fi tip jar already covers the same kind of support. |

**Corrected 1 Oct 2026:** both dates above assume about 4 net subscribers a day, close to the last 7 days' pace (3.86 a day, a 12-upload week). That pace is the optimistic one. On the last 28 days' pace (2.25 a day) the expanded tier lands 26 Mar 2027 and the full tier 3 Nov 2027, and no pace the channel has run puts 1,000 subscribers before 8 Apr 2027, so mid-May 2027 is the best case, not the middle (30 Sept post-mortem, 102 subscribers read on 30 Sept).

**Assumptions:** the same revenue-per-1,000-views range as the competitor estimate, $1–3.50, for a young international gaming audience. Shorts add almost nothing. Our videos are over 8 minutes, so mid-roll ads can run, which helps. 62% of our views are muted autoplay previews, which do not show ads, and that pulls us toward the low end. Earnings scale with views: MCSR Matches gets 150–300K views a month and earns about $150–1,000 by the same method.

**1 February 2027 does not bind (corrected 1 Oct 2026)**

The last 7 days (3.86 a day, a 12-upload week) give 21 May 2027, which is optimistic. The last 28 days (2.25 a day) give 3 Nov 2027. ## How do we increase our subscriber count?

Asked 29 Sept 2026 (07:30 UTC), answered 29 Sept 2026 (07:35 UTC), after three parallel analyses (which videos brought subscribers, what the pipeline already does, what benchmarks and the competitor research and two channel audits say). Data: our own Analytics, 9 Aug–29 Sept.

**Where subscribers come from**
- Long videos, not Shorts. Long-form brings 1.43 subs per 1,000 views (66 subs from 46K views). Shorts bring 0.21 (2 from 9.6K); 11 of the 12 Shorts brought none.
- A few matches do most of the work. edcr vs doogile alone brought 26 subs (26 of the first 49 long-form subscribers), and the best converters by rate feature doogile (TAS).
- The gap (corrected 1 Oct 2026): the 29 Sept answer set a target of 1.8 subs per 1,000 views, which is 7.3 a day at about 4,000 views a day, to reach 1,000 subscribers by 1 Feb 2027. That date does not bind (see the monetisation answer above), and 1.8 is about twice what the channel has run: 1.05 gained per 1,000 views in the week of 14 to 20 Sept and 0.98 in 21 to 27 Sept (30 Sept post-mortem). Only the weeks of 24 Aug and 31 Aug held it (7.01 and 5.43), both under 3,700 views.
- 32 of our 100 subs did not come from any video, most likely from the channel page, which has one section today.
- Conversion fell as reach grew: 4.3, 2.5, 0.96 subs per 1,000 views, week by week.

**The plan, most subs for the effort first, and who does each**
1. Publish a long video every day, no gaps. 12 of 29 days this month had nothing going public, all before 22 Sept, and every competitor stopped growing when it stopped posting. The only thing that blocks an upload is unsaved hooks, so save several at once, and raise `nightlyMaxRenders` to 2 before being away. Operator: hooks and a setting.
2. Add end screens pointing at that rivalry's playlist (or edcr vs doogile), plus a subscribe button. None exist today. Operator, in Studio, about 1 minute per video, until Studio automation is built.
3. Pick recognisable players more often: a suggestion-scoring bonus for edcr, doogile, Feinberg, Infume, Skycrab, lowkey and silverrruns, at least four in rotation. Claude builds it; it changes what the nightly picks, so it needs the operator's yes.
4. Give the subscribe ask a reason: change the bare "SUBSCRIBE" on the cards to something like "A NEW MATCH EVERY DAY · SUBSCRIBE". Neither the description nor the pinned comment asks at all yet; add the same line to both. Claude builds it, the operator approves the wording ("yes" means use that line).
5. Keep more people past the first minute: 16–22 of every 100 viewers are lost early. Cut the intro card from 5 s to 3 s and turn on the COMING UP teaser during the countdown (`countdownTeaser`, currently off). The video that converts best per view is also the one people watch most of. Operator: two settings.
6. Build out the channel page: a popular-uploads section and the busiest rivalry playlists. Operator, in Studio.
7. Post fast when something big happens: queue a crossover or big-name match that night and publish the next day, not at the next free slot. Operator, when it happens.

**Not worth the time:** polishing Shorts for subscribers (keep them for reach only); judging the mid-video SUBSCRIBE line yet (only 5 days of data, look again in mid-October); chasing raw views.

**How we will know:** two numbers weekly from the bot's `/stats`: subs per 1,000 views and days with a video (target 7 of 7). The 1.8 target of 29 Sept is dropped; read each week against the last two full weeks, 1.05 and 0.98 gained per 1,000 views (14 to 27 Sept, read 30 Sept). Channels under 500 subscribers get extra algorithm help now, so sooner is better.

**Still waiting on the operator:** a go on items 3 and 4 (and the wording for 4), and his own Studio and settings steps.

## Should we post clips of speedrunners' statements about the edcr situation?

Asked 29 Sept 2026 (07:37 UTC), answered 29 Sept 2026 (07:39 UTC). The operator overrode the advice at about 07:50 UTC and the video was built and scheduled the same day.

**What happened** (MCSR Ranked's official post on X, https://x.com/MCSR_Ranked/status/2103194125361467657, plus reports around it): around mid-September, chat logs surfaced showing edcr repeatedly using racial and transphobic slurs. MCSR Ranked checked them, disqualified him from the Season 11 Playoffs (why his quarterfinal against Feinberg was a walkover) and asked for his side. He first denied writing the messages; two days later he posted a public statement taking accountability. Contacted again after the playoffs, he briefly apologised and admitted he had been dishonest with them. Final decision: edcr is permanently banned from future Playoffs. The post covers Playoffs only. Later research (28 Sept): he is still playing ranked and is #1 on the ladder at 2402 elo.

**The advice given: no to drama clips**
1. Monetisation risk: clips of other people's statements, tweets and stream moments with no original contribution are what YouTube's reused-content review rejects, and that review is already our weak spot. We were about 100 days from applying.
2. Ads and policy: a video about a real person's slurs and ban lands in YouTube's "controversial issues" category, which typically gets limited or no ads. It also invites pile-ons in the comments, which the harassment rules watch; one of our edcr matches already has an argument between two sides.
3. We depend on the runners: the channel uses players' POVs without a formal agreement (MCSR has no policy either way). Clipping runners' statements about a peer's scandal risks their goodwill or takedown requests.
4. It would not bring subscribers: subs come from full replays of known players (1.43 per 1,000 views), not clips (Shorts 0.21). None of the three competitors grew from drama; they grew by posting often and from big creators playing ranked.

**What was advised instead:** stay neutral on edcr's ranked matches, with every hook about the match, never the situation. Whether edcr stays on the "recognisable players" list is the operator's call (he is our best converter, edcr vs doogile brought 26 subs, but featuring him heavily now is a stance people will read into). Turn on "Hold potentially inappropriate comments for review" in Studio, Settings, Community. Speedrunning clips only make sense with our own commentary on top.

**The operator's decision:** he would still post it: it is controversial and will bring a few people, and the worst case is a single video that is not monetised in the future. Follow-up findings: edcr's statement (Pastebin copy) says he wrote the messages at 15–16 in another game's community, that he is 19 now, and "I'm sorry to everyone that I hurt". Ludwig and skye have argued publicly for a path back or an appeal after a year rather than a permanent ban. Feinberg Daily's 8:21 upload of Feinberg's statement has 83K views since 22 Sept; other coverage has 13–35K each. Twitch clips max out at 60 seconds, so the 8-minute piece is a section of Feinberg's past broadcast. He keeps copyright on his stream, so a takedown could bring a copyright strike; crediting him on screen and linking his channel lowers the risk. A second option was drafted: a 3-minute explainer (own on-screen timeline, quote cards, 15 seconds each of Feinberg and infume, ending "permanent, or a path back?").

## Do competitors' subscribers grow exponentially? (9 Oct 2026)

No. Every MCSR replay channel grew in bursts tied to heavy posting plus an outside moment, then flattened, with no
compounding. Counts are from the Data API on 9 Oct; the history comes from the 28 Sept research (Wayback captures, `research/competitors-2026-09-28/`).
- **MCSR Vault** (9,310): ~50/day Nov 2025–Apr 2026, peak 109/day during Ludwig's MCSR arc (27 Dec–4 Jan); 3–15/day
  since mid-April as posting dropped; +~20 in 28 Sept–9 Oct.
- **MCSR Replays** (3,060): most of its subs in Jan–Feb 2026 (peak 46.5/day); silent since 18 Apr; flat since (3,060 on
  28 Sept and today).
- **mcsrmatches** (1,670, 139 videos): nearly all of its first ~1,060 in the Ludwig window; ~1.1/day after a 4-month
  break; 1,640 → 1,670 in 11 days (~2.7/day) now.
- **Us** (129 on 9 Oct, 123 on 7 Oct): ~3–4/day, already level with mcsrmatches in absolute terms.
What drives the curve: posting volume (growth stops when posting stops) and catching an outside attention moment
(a big streamer's arc, a viral match), never official events covered late.
