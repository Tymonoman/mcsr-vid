# Who watches MCSR Replayoffs, and how to make them invested (24 Sept 2026)

Read-only research for the operator's goal: "we need people to be invested into speedruning. figure ou what's our target and build onto it." Nothing was written to the repo, /media, the config or YouTube.

## The short version

- **Two audiences, and they behave differently.** 74% of views come from the Home feed. Those viewers mostly leave early and subscribe at **2.0 per 1k engaged views**. The videos that YouTube also placed in the next-video column (19–42% Suggested traffic) subscribe at **6.4 per 1k engaged views** and watch longer (4.5 vs 3.8 min per engaged view).
- **Who they are:** 92.5% male, 76% aged 18–34 (25–34 is the biggest group at 42%), 14.5% aged 13–17. 49% watch on desktop. 25% are in the US, 35% in the four big English-speaking countries, and about 17% in India and South-east Asia. 98.2% of views come from people who are not subscribed.
- **"Views" overstate the audience.** 42% of all views (13,205) are silent autoplays in the Home feed that last about 5 s each. The people who actually watched come to 15,087 watch-page views at about 3 min each.
- **Across the niche, two things beat match replays by a wide margin: explaining the game and famous faces.** The official MCSR Ranked channel's 8 "Explanations" videos have a median of 39.5k views, against 4.7k for its 185 playoff and match VODs. MCSR Vault's 11 videos with Ludwig, Grian or Schlatt have a median of 51k, against 9.9k for the rest of its channel.
- **Primary target:** the Minecraft regular who watches Minecraft YouTube but not MCSR Ranked. That's where the views are, and it's who the goal is about.
- **Secondary target:** the MCSR follower. They subscribe at three times the rate, and they're the ones who get the videos placed next to other MCSR content.
- **The 12 levers below come down to three changes:** teach the race while it's on screen, make the two players into people with a history, and give the viewer a stake (a guess, a side, a next meeting). None of them names a winner.

### What the commenters show (50 threads)

Verbatim comments are left out of this copy on purpose; what they showed, in the report's own reading:

- **The regulars write in jargon** about routes, crafting and specific runs.
- **Newcomers ask how it works** (for example why players lower their render distance in the fortress), which is the case for explaining the moves.
- **Faces carry personality:** comments react to a player's expression at a moment in the run.
- **Nickname hooks split the room:** one nickname hook drew the most likes of any comment (+22), while several others asked who the nickname meant or objected to it. A title should not depend on a nickname the audience does not share.
- **Trust:** one comment (+6, 4 replies) said the channel reads as AI-generated and that the comments and description are the weak part; the overlay itself was praised. The channel's only replies at the time were one identical template pinned on 12 videos.
- **The overlay lands:** a clean-overlay comment drew +12, and the request for one streamer's audio on one side has since shipped.

### What gets views in the niche

These are ratios within the same channel, so channel size cancels out.

1. **Explaining the game:** official Explanations have 8x the median of the official match VODs (39.5k vs 4.7k). Couriway's guides reach 273k–1.4M.
2. **Famous faces from outside MCSR:** 5x on MCSR Vault (51k vs 9.9k); its top three are Ludwig videos (356k, 225k, 200k). The official "Ludwig vs Fan Show Match" drew 30k against a 4.9k median. Couriway's coaching-YouTubers series has 1.2M, 286k and 220k.
3. **Records and their stories:** the Skycrab world record fed Couriway (318k) and Lewis Fulham (88k). "The run that knocked Feinberg off #1" got 16k against Fulham's 13k median.
4. **Money and stakes:** the official Short "Risks $1500 on Final Match" has 399k against a 22.5k median.
5. **Match replays on their own:** 2–11k. That's our lane today.

**One non-finding:** the official Shorts with question titles ("Will Doogile Win This Match with 10 Apples?") have a median of 20k, against 24k for the others. A question isn't a reach lever on its own. We use it because it doesn't spoil.

### The wider pool that could become fans

- **Minecraft YouTube:** Feinberg and Couriway have 600k subscribers between them, with multi-million-view videos.
- **The 2020–21 speedrun wave:** Minecraft speedrunning "became popular and mainstream around 2020" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minecraft_speedrunning)).
- **Our own data:** YouTube's Home feed is already putting us in front of this group: about 23k Home-feed views in 28 days, 13k of them 5-second previews. The job is to convert them.

## 3. What turns spectators into invested fans (evidence)

| Driver | Evidence | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Understanding** | [Hamari & Sjöblom 2017](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/intr-04-2016-0085/full/html): acquiring knowledge about the game is one of four motives that predict how often people watch esports. The others are escapism, novelty and athlete aggressiveness. The official channel's explainers get 8x its match VODs. [Summoning Salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summoning_Salt) built 1.5M+ subscribers by turning record progressions into stories that outsiders can follow. | A cold viewer who can't read the split board leaves in the overworld. That fits our 16–22-point drop between 3% and 10% of every video. |
| **Narrative / rivalry** | [Bryant, Brown, Comisky & Zillmann 1982](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1460-2466.1982.tb00482.x): the same tennis footage was enjoyed more when the commentary framed the players as rivals rather than friends. Drive to Survive: 22% of US F1 fans called it a "major reason" they became fans, and more than half said it played some role ([Morning Consult](https://pro.morningconsult.com/analysis/drive-to-survive-f1-popularity-new-fans)). | The story sits outside the footage and costs nothing to add. Our H2H counter already holds rivalry data. |
| **Underdogs and uncertainty** | [Vandello, Goldschmied & Richards 2007](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0146167207307488): people back whoever is framed as the underdog, and credit them with more effort. [Ryu et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10481709/), 629 LCK highlight videos: upsets got +8.2% views; uncertain matchups got more views and comments; higher combined Elo got more views; evening games got +16% views. [Petersen-Wagner et al. 2025](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/12267/): uncertainty of outcome raises YouTube football-highlight demand. | Frame the question, show the gap, and never answer it before the end. |
| **Personalities** | Our own search traffic is 67% player names. Commenters react to faces. Across the niche, famous people are the biggest multiplier (5x on MCSR Vault). Chess: influencer events (PogChamps) pushed Twitch chess to 376k concurrent viewers, and GothamChess reached 8.0M subscribers ([Stream Hatchet](https://streamhatchet.com/blog/live-streamings-effect-on-chess-popularity/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levy_Rozman)). | Viewers attach to people. A name with no identity attached is just a word to them. |
| **Belonging / prediction** | GothamChess's [Guess the Elo](https://eloguessr.net/blog/history-of-guess-the-elo) is his signature series: viewers commit to a guess, then see the reveal. Fantasy-sports players watch more games ([Nesbit & King 2010](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227620976_The_Impact_of_Fantasy_Sports_on_Television_Viewership)), and NFL games with more fantasy-relevant starters draw higher Nielsen ratings ([Fortunato](https://trace.tennessee.edu/jasm/vol3/iss1/16/)). | Once a viewer has made a guess, they have a reason to stay for the ending. We get 1.2 comments per 1k views, so there's plenty of room. |
| **Stakes** | Money and "final match" framing is the top official Short at 18x median. Game quality (Elo) raises views in the LCK data. | Rank, elo and the playoffs are our version of stakes. They have to be said in words in the first seconds, not left as numbers on a card. |

## 4. Target personas

### Primary: the Minecraft regular (Home feed)

**Who he is:**
- A man aged 18–34, on a Windows PC in the evening.
- In the US, UK, Canada or Australia, or in India or South-east Asia.
- Grew up on Minecraft and still watches it on YouTube: Feinberg's tournaments, Couriway's "how fast can I" videos, the Skycrab world-record reaction.
- Knows what a bastion, blaze rods and an eye of ender are. Maybe watched Dream-era speedruns in 2020.
- Has never played Ranked. Doesn't know what elo means here or what a good nether entry is. Might recognise Feinberg and no one else.

**How he behaves on our channel:**
- YouTube shows him our thumbnail, and half the time he gets a 5-second silent preview.
- If he clicks, he watches about 3.8 minutes and leaves in the overworld.
- He subscribes at 2.0 per 1k engaged views.

**What he needs from the video:**
- Why these two, and why this match.
- What they're doing right now, and whether it's good.
- Someone to root for, and a reason to come back.

He is about three-quarters of our views, he is the "people" in "make people invested", and he is the pool Feinberg and Couriway already proved exists.

### Secondary: the MCSR follower (Suggested, search, channel page)

**Who he is:**
- Plays or has played Ranked, is in the 70k-member Discord, watches Feinberg or Infume live.
- Missed this match, or wants both POVs.

**How he finds and uses us:**
- Arrives from the next-video column beside other MCSR uploads, from a player-name search, or from our channel page.
- Comments in jargon and catches wrong stats ("I'm pretty sure some of the stats are wrong").
- Watches 4.5 minutes and subscribes at 6.4 per 1k engaged views, three times the primary.

**What he needs:**
- The match soon after it happens.
- Stats that are right.
- The rivalry remembered.
- Nothing that talks down to him.

**Why he matters beyond his own views:** he produces the comments and watch-next sessions that tell YouTube who else to show the video to.

### Not a target yet

- **The Shorts-feed scroller:** 0 subscribers from 3,197 views. Serve him only as a route to the long-form.
- **General esports fans with no Minecraft background:** too far from us. The on-ramp for them is crossover videos, which are other people's.

### Target statement, version 2 (improves the night-1 draft)

> MCSR Replayoffs is for people who grew up on Minecraft and still watch it on YouTube. Most of them are men aged 18 to 34 watching on a PC in the evening. They have seen a speedrun but never followed MCSR Ranked. Every day we post one whole race between two of the best runners in the world: both players' own streams side by side, synced to the frame, uncut, and never spoiled. Before the countdown ends, they know who the two players are and why this match matters. During the race, they can tell what each phase is for and whether a split is good. By the finish, they have picked a side, made a guess and have a reason to watch the next meeting. The MCSR regulars already know the players and find us through the next-video column and player searches. They get the same race with the stats right and the rivalry remembered.

### What "invested" means, and how to measure it

A viewer is invested when they can name a runner and pick a side, can tell whether a split is good, and come back for the next meeting.

| Proxy | Now | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Subs per 1k engaged views on Home-feed videos | 2.0 | 4 |
| Comments per 1k views | 1.2 | 3 |
| Player-name search views per month | ~150 | rising |
| Retention loss between 3% and 10% | 16–22 points | under 12 |
| Subscribers from no specific video (mostly the channel page) | 28 of 77 | tracked |

## 5. Twelve levers

"Operator's call" marks anything that changes what a nightly render outputs, or touches the live channel (house rule). Effort: S is under half a day, M is 1–2 days, L is more than that.

### Understanding

**1. Phase captions: tell the cold viewer what the race is.**
- **Change:**
  - The meta column shows one plain line as a player enters each phase. For example: "NETHER: blaze rods from a fortress, pearls from piglins", "STRONGHOLD: throw eyes, find the portal", "END: kill the dragon".
  - A static glossary of about six lines, written once by hand.
  - Adds stills in `splitStates.ts`, the same mechanism as `midRollFramesOf`. No per-frame rendering, so render time barely changes.
- **Evidence:**
  - The 16–22-point loss between 3% and 10% of the video happens in the overworld, straight after the intro.
  - Hamari & Sjöblom's knowledge motive.
  - Official explainers get 8x.
  - The "why lower render distance" comment.
- **Effort:** S–M. Operator's call. Test it on 5 videos alongside the "COMING UP" teaser already in the teaser worktree (dd66022). It's the night-1 judge's "narrative layer", which I'd move to first place.

**2. Benchmarks: show what "good" looks like.**
- **Change:**
  - Under each split time, a grey reference such as "top-100 median 2:05".
  - Computed once from the matches in the suggestion cache, so no new API load.
  - Stills only.
- **Evidence:**
  - Guess the Elo works because the viewer has a scale to judge against.
  - Regulars read the gaps ("fein was ahead like 2 secs"). A newcomer can't read an absolute time.
- **Effort:** M. Operator's call.
- **Note:** the night-1 judge said NO to "pace vs own average" because nobody had asked for it. This is different: a fixed yardstick for the newcomer. Judge it by retention at the first split (~90 s), not by comments.

**3. "How to watch an MCSR race": trailer, pinned Short and end-screen target.**
- **Change:**
  - 60–90 s with no voice: the overlay's parts labelled, the five phases, what elo and head-to-head mean, and the promise ("whole race, both POVs, never spoiled").
  - Built as a Remotion composition from existing parts.
  - The operator sets it as the channel trailer for non-subscribers, puts it first on the Home tab, and cuts a Short from it.
- **Evidence:**
  - 98.2% of our views come from non-subscribers.
  - Channel-page visitors watch 3.5 min per view.
  - **36% of subscribers (28 of 77) came from no specific video.**
  - Official explainers get 26–106k; Couriway's guides 273k–1.4M.
- **Effort:** M, one-off. Operator's call (live channel). A text-only version avoids the voice and identity question the judge raised.

### Personalities

**4. One line on who each player is.**
- **Change:**
  - A hand-written `playerFacts` map for the ~20 regulars, shown on the intro card under the name and as one line in the description.
  - Facts only, each with a date. For example: "Skycrab: world record 6:39, July 2026"; "hackingnoises: 2026 World Champion"; "Feinberg: #1 English Minecraft streamer on Twitch"; "lowkey: held the record at 6:50".
- **Evidence:**
  - 67% of our search views name a player.
  - Viewers attach to faces ("dead inside", "so orange").
  - Bryant et al.: framing the players changes how much viewers enjoy the same footage.
  - Stories like Skycrab's record, "a year after the 6:17 choke", are what records content runs on.
- **Effort:** S code plus 30 minutes of operator writing. Operator's call.
- **Guard:** no model-written bios (the judge's NO stands), nothing about private lives.

**5. Explain the nickname in the hook.**
- **Change:**
  - Keep the operator's register ("YN vs TAS", "UNC vs OG").
  - Add an optional one-line gloss per side, typed next to the hook, shown on the intro card. For example: "TAS: what chat calls doogile's machine-like precision".
- **Evidence:**
  - The comments split: insiders liked the nickname hook (+22), everyone else asked who it meant or objected to it.
  - The hook gets the click. The gloss is what turns it into a personality instead of an in-joke.
- **Effort:** S (a hook-form field plus an intro-card line). Operator's call.

**6. Crossover matches go first.**
- **Change:**
  - Add a `crossoverPlayers` weight in `src/dashboard/suggest.ts`.
  - When someone with an audience outside MCSR plays Ranked or a private room with a top runner, that match rises to the top. Examples: Ludwig (account `ludwigahgren`), or a YouTuber coached by Couriway.
  - The big MCSR names, Feinberg and Couriway, get a smaller boost.
- **Evidence:**
  - MCSR Vault's crossover videos have a 5x median.
  - The official Ludwig showmatch drew 30k.
  - Couriway's coaching series has 1.2M.
  - PogChamps did the same for chess.
- **Effort:** S. Operator's call (it changes what the nightly picks). Limited by whether both POVs exist.

### Narrative and stakes

**7. The rivalry chain: every repeat meeting links to the last one.**
- **Change:**
  - Add a description line "last time they met: <link>", read from the matchup's previous `youtube.json`. Never the result.
  - The end screen points to the previous meeting or the matchup playlist. This is set by hand in Studio; the API can't set end screens.
